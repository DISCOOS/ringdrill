Three exercise modes, and station-owned durations

ADR-0062 proposed an authored rounds: list. This is the alternative: the author states how teams relate to posts (one field, three options) and how long a station takes (where that station is edited). The app keeps doing the arithmetic — every clock face below is derived, none of it is typed, and all of it is the round table the brief already renders.

2 · The mode field open. Three options, each one sentence — the app has to teach this, so the sentence is the design.

Conduct
Ring Route
One team per station. Teams rotate; the app works out who is where.
Together
One station, all teams. Everyone works the same station, then moves on together.
Split
Anything in between. You group the stations that run together and say which teams go to each. Groups need not be the same size.
All three are the same thing with different group sizes — one team per group, all teams in one group, or any mix. The first two are generated, which is why they cost the author nothing; Split is where assignment is actually authored. Durations are a separate question, answered per station — see 4.

A group holds any number of stations, and a station any number of teams. Two teams here, one there, one there. When every group holds exactly one team this is a ring drill round, which is why Ring Route is a preset rather than a different mechanism — see the note at the bottom. The codes 7c–7e stay derived from position (ADR-0059), which is the whole reason not to split the exercise into several.

The same two checks run for a document written by hand: analyze reports the collision as an error and the unplaced team as a warning, so a YAML or MCP author is not told less than the app tells.

Switching away from Ring Route or Together costs nothing, because neither stored anything the author typed. Switching away from Split does, and the difference is worth stating rather than having one generic “are you sure”.

What the author types, in total

  • One mode per exercise — a choice from three, defaulting to today’s behaviour.
  • An execution time on a station, only where it differs from the exercise’s.
  • In Split only: a parallel group, and which teams go to which station in it — any number of stations, any number of teams each, groups of unequal size. This is real authoring work, and it is the only place in the design where the author assigns anything by hand, because it is a decision rather than a computation.

No round list, no per-round entries, no clock faces. Every time above is derived, and the uniform exercise — most of a course day — is untouched by all of it.

One model, three presets

There is only one thing underneath: a round is a set of groups, and a group is one station with some teams on it. The three modes are group sizes.

  • Ring Route — every group holds one team, and there are as many groups as stations. The rotation is generated.
  • Together — one group holds every team. Generated too.
  • Split — any number of groups, of any size, and they need not match each other. Four teams across three stations is 2 + 1 + 1. Authored.

So Ring Route is the case where every split is a single team, and Together is the case where there is one split holding all of them — neither is a different mechanism. They stay separate modes because they are the two that can be generated, and generating them is what keeps the common case free: an author who picks Ring Route assigns nothing at all, which is the whole reason not to expose the group model directly and call it a day.

This also says where the effort belongs. Authoring cost rises with irregularity and nowhere else: nothing for a ring drill, nothing for an all-together exercise, and one assignment per team only in the case that is genuinely irregular. That is the opposite of rounds:, which charged every exercise for the existence of the odd one.

One representation, not two

The schedule is shown as the round table the brief already renders ({{exercise.roundTable}}, rotationRoundTable), not as a new chart. All three modes are the same table; Together and Split add a Station column, because in those modes a round is a station or a group of them. An author who has read a brief has already read this table, and there is one implementation to keep true rather than two.

Is “rounds of different lengths” a fourth mode?

No — and mostly because it is already here. A round’s length is the execution time of the stations live in it, so unequal rounds fall out wherever the stations differ: Together gives one round per station, which is exactly Exercise 7’s 70 / 100 / 75 (panel 8), and Split gives one round per group. Only Ring Route keeps its rounds equal, and necessarily so: every station is live in every round, so the longest one sets all of them (panel 5).

Two things still cannot be said, and neither is a mode:

  • The same station taking different times on different visits — 30 minutes the first time through, 15 on the repeat.
  • Phase times varying by round — a longer evaluation on the last round than on the first.

The mode axis is which teams are on which stations. Duration is a different axis, and an orthogonal one: you could want either of those in Ring Route, in Together or in Split. Putting a duration concept in the mode enum would mix the two, and it would be the only mode that cannot be generated — which is the property that makes the other three cost the author nothing.

If a real plan needs them, the shape is a per-round override that composes with the mode: the mode still decides who is where, and the override adjusts that round’s phases. Which is ADR-0062’s Option C, argued against on ergonomic grounds and deliberately left unbuilt — the booklet that produced three failing exercises did not need it, and “real plans are the specification” is one of this ADR’s own drivers. The door is here, and this paragraph is the handle.

Compared with ADR-0062’s rounds: list

rounds: asks the author to translate what they know (“station b takes 100 minutes”) into round-space (“round 2 is 100 minutes”) by hand, and to enumerate every round to say one thing about one of them. In the editor that is an unbounded list with inheritance rules to explain. Here the two facts are stated where they already live, and the round structure falls out of them.

What it costs

  • Mode and per-station duration reach the archive. Neither is recoverable from the derived schedule, so this needs an ADR-0059 migration rung — where a timing-only rounds: would have needed none.
  • Rotation math changes rather than accumulates. Mode alters team-to-station assignment, which is the part ADR-0062 flagged as the half that grows.
  • Waiting becomes visible. Panel 5 is honest, not comfortable: an author who did not realise their stations were unequal will see idle time they did not know they had.
